## Overview

The Jackalope is a mythical creature consisting of a jackrabbit with antelope or deer antlers, created as a taxidermy hoax in Douglas, Wyoming in the 1930s by brothers Douglas and Ralph Herrick.

After a hunting trip, the brothers mounted a jackrabbit head with deer antlers and sold it to a local hotel, launching a phenomenon that spread across America through postcards, souvenirs, and roadside attractions.

The tongue-in-cheek legend attributes various abilities to the Jackalope including mimicking human voices and producing milk with medicinal properties, while claiming it's aggressive and dangerous. Despite being a known hoax, the Jackalope has become an iconic symbol of American West folklore and tall tale tradition.

Douglas, Wyoming embraced the creature as the "Jackalope Capital," erecting statues and selling hunting licenses. Interestingly, the Shope papillomavirus does cause horn-like growths on rabbits, providing a medical basis for the legend.

## Scientific Basis: Shope Papillomavirus

### Real "Horned Rabbits"

The **Shope papillomavirus** provides medical basis for horned rabbit legends:

**Discovery**: Dr. Richard Shope, 1933

**Effects**:
- Causes keratinous carcinomas (horn-like growths) on rabbits
- Growths can appear on head, resembling horns
- Particularly affects cottontail rabbits
- Creates genuinely horned rabbits in rare cases

**Folklore Connection**:
- May have inspired earlier European horned hare legends
- Provides scientific explanation for "impossible" creature
- Historical specimens exist in museums
- Demonstrates nature can be stranger than fiction
